Choosing restaurant POS software in Pakistan starts with a practical question: can your team complete a normal service, including the awkward orders, without losing track of what happened? A feature list helps you shortlist systems. A repeatable demonstration helps you decide whether one fits your restaurant.

This guide is for owners comparing a first POS or replacing an existing system. It is a buying method, not a certification of any vendor's hardware, tax integration or offline capability.

## 1. Write a one-page operating brief

List your order sources: counter, phone, website, branded app and any marketplace. Record how many branches you operate, who takes orders, who prepares them and who closes the shift. Separate what you need today from a possible future expansion.

Bring a sample menu with a required modifier, an unavailable item and a price that differs between branches, if that reflects your operation. Use sample customer details for testing rather than real customers' personal information.

### Identify the handoffs that cause rework

Choose one recurring problem to investigate: a counter order that reaches preparation late, an online order that is retyped, or a rider payment that is difficult to reconcile. Describe the steps and the people involved. Avoid turning the brief into a long list of features nobody will use.

## 2. Run the same demonstration with each vendor

Ask the vendor to follow your sample order from entry to the final record. Start with a counter order, then compare an online order. Look for a clear order reference, correct item options, the intended branch and an understandable preparation handoff.

- Enter an order with a required item choice and check the price.
- Find an online order and identify who is responsible for it.
- Change preparation status and review what staff can see.
- Review pickup or delivery according to your operation.
- Follow a cancellation or correction into the report.

Record whether each requirement was demonstrated, promised for later or outside scope. A presentation screenshot is useful context, but it does not show how your staff will handle an exception.

## 3. Verify devices and integrations explicitly

Share computer, printer and payment-device model numbers. If your restaurant requires a kitchen display, marketplace import, accounting export or fiscal integration, name the provider and the actions you expect it to perform. Request a demonstration and written scope.

### Test interruptions as well as normal service

Ask what happens when the connection drops, a device restarts or two staff members handle the same order. An offline claim should be demonstrated with the intended devices, including how records reconcile after reconnecting. Do not assume every cloud POS works offline or that a supported printer means every printer will work.

For tax or invoicing requirements, obtain advice appropriate to your business and verify the exact integration with the provider. This guide does not establish which obligations apply to your restaurant.

## 4. Include stock, riders and branches in the review

For [inventory management](/services/inventory-management), use your own stock units and ask how opening balances and adjustments are recorded. If recipe deductions, expiry dates or branch transfers are essential, test them individually. Basic inventory support does not establish those capabilities.

For [delivery management](/services/delivery-management), follow rider assignment, status changes and end-of-shift reconciliation. Test tracking on the intended rider phone and agree how staff respond when location or connectivity is unavailable.

For more than one branch, compare menus, availability, staff access and reporting scope. Bring the actual delivery boundaries to the [Pakistan setup discussion](/locations). A multi-branch label does not explain which configuration or commercial terms your business needs.

## 5. Compare the complete cost over a useful period

Ask each vendor for setup, recurring software, hardware, payment, app-store and specialist development costs separately. Confirm branch coverage, support, data access and cancellation terms. Use the same sales and branch assumptions for every quote.

Restrova's owner-approved model is PKR 10,000 onboarding once, followed by the higher of PKR 5,000 and 1% of monthly sales processed through Restrova. At PKR 500,000 processed sales the monthly service fee is PKR 5,000. At PKR 800,000 it is PKR 8,000. It is not a PKR 5,000 charge plus 1%.

These examples cover the stated software formula, not a complete operating budget. Confirm taxes, payment charges, branch scope and exclusions. Use the [pricing calculator](/pricing) and keep the proposal alongside your comparison notes.

## 6. Check reporting before you rely on it

Ask which period, branches and order statuses a sales report includes. Trace a completed order and an adjusted order into the totals. Review the handling of cancellations, refunds and discounts before comparing reports with cash or monthly billing.

Sales are not profit. Food costs, wages, rent, packaging and delivery may be recorded elsewhere. Confirm the [reporting and export requirements](/services/reporting-analytics) you actually need instead of assuming a dashboard replaces accounting.

## 7. Agree a controlled rollout

Name the person responsible for the menu, devices, staff training and opening stock. Agree a test service, the issues that must be resolved before launch and a fallback process if staff cannot complete a critical task. Confirm how the team will get help and who can approve changes.

Evaluate success against your starting problem: whether orders reach the right team, staff can find their records and closing questions can be resolved. Avoid treating a busier promotion or a new dashboard as proof that the software increased profit.

A stock routine begins with a count people can repeat. Choose a manageable set of items, agree the unit for each and nominate the person responsible. If purchasing uses cartons and counting uses bottles, record the conversion clearly. Mixing units can create an apparent shortage even when the physical stock is correct.

Item availability and ingredient-level recipe deductions are separate capabilities; do not assume one proves the other.

A useful software price explains both the calculation and its boundaries. Separate the one-time setup amount from recurring service, then identify costs that depend on your particular restaurant. Keep the proposal available when comparing options.

Restrova's approved onboarding fee is PKR 10,000 once. Each month, calculate 1% of sales processed through Restrova and compare it with PKR 5,000. The monthly service fee is whichever amount is higher. The two amounts are not added together, and sales handled entirely outside Restrova are not the stated basis of this calculation.

The threshold is PKR 500,000 of processed monthly sales because 1% of that amount is PKR 5,000. At PKR 200,000, the monthly fee remains PKR 5,000. At PKR 800,000, it becomes PKR 8,000. With onboarding and one month at that second level, the stated amounts total PKR 18,000 before any unconfirmed additional charges. The minimum applies even when processed sales are zero.

Ask what the proposal includes for your branch count, devices, app-store accounts, payment processing and any custom development. Taxes and other exclusions need confirmation. Also agree the billing start date and how refunds, cancellations and adjustments affect processed sales; a calculator cannot determine those contractual details.

Compare the complete cost over a consistent period and with the same sales assumptions. Keep the operational costs of delivery, packaging and customer acquisition separate from the software component. A smaller software bill is not by itself proof of a more profitable restaurant.
